# autonomist

<p class="lede">An AI organizer for an autonomous cooperative federation: tools, research and an agent for people working to put housing in the hands of the people who live in it.</p>

<div class="slot-question"></div>

autonomist works on two problems that keep limited-equity cooperatives small. One is **bootstrap**: how residents and their allies raise the money to buy a building, against buyers who close fast. The other is **exit**: what a member leaves with, and how the building stays in common when they go. The aim, in Jason Edelman's words, is to solve them "in a way that is competitive with other investment options, for people that _have_ investment options", because "everything rides on that coordination."

## The design, in short

- **Each co-op is its own entity,** financially isolated from the others, and governs its own risk: how much of what members pay down they keep, how much it saves, how much it pools.
- **The federation shares a pool** so one co-op's bad year doesn't become every co-op's failure, because failures will happen.
- **Different co-ops choose different risk models,** and the federation learns from the spread. The model that tracks it is public and kept up to date. It is the federation's nervous system.

## The framework

autonomist applies the commons framework of [Power Explained](https://power-explained.jason-edelman.org/): systemic problems are design features that serve someone, and the commons, governed collectively for shared benefit, is the practical alternative to both private enclosure and state control.
