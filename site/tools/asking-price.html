What the asking price means for rent

When a building is listed for sale, its price says something about what the next owner will need from it. This tool works that out from two numbers on the listing.

Loan terms (ordinary bank terms by default)

How to read it

A buyer who pays the asking price with an ordinary loan needs the building's income to cover the loan payments with room to spare; lenders usually want about 1.25 times. If today's income doesn't do that, the buyer's plan needs more income: usually higher rents, as homes turn over or leases renew. This is not a scheduled increase. A buyer with more cash, or content with a thinner return, needs less. But it is the arithmetic the listing is pricing in.

What to do with it

  • Talk with your neighbors first. One household asking questions is a complaint; a building asking together is an organization.
  • Ask what the listing shows. The broker's offering memorandum usually has the rent roll and expenses a buyer is shown.
  • Know the law where you are. In Virginia, the landlord-tenant act lists organizing or joining a tenant organization among the acts a landlord may not retaliate against (Va. Code § 55.1-1258; a new version takes effect January 1, 2027, so read the current text). If a building is converted to condominiums, month-to-month tenants get 120 days' notice and a 60-day exclusive right to buy their own unit at the offering price (Va. Code § 55.1-1982). Virginia gives tenants of an ordinary apartment building no right to buy it when it's sold. A tenant-rights group or lawyer can tell you more.
