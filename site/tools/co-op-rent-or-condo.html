Co-op, rent or condo?

Three households start with the same savings and the same housing budget. One rents, one buys a condo, one joins a limited-equity co-op. Whatever a household doesn't spend on housing, it invests. Who is ahead after some years?

What's inside

The co-op is the model's worked 8-unit building with its default governance: members are credited the loan paydown and their share is indexed at 3% a year; 5% of market rent goes to reserves and the federation pool. The condo buyer puts 20% down plus 3% closing costs, pays property tax at Norfolk's $1.23 per $100 (the City's rate effective July 2024), a $450 monthly fee and 1% of value a year in upkeep, and pays 7% to sell. Building values move with rents and costs. No income taxes are modeled. The full tables are in Results, section 3.
