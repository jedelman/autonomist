Federation stress lab

Twenty-four limited-equity co-ops, fifteen years, random shocks: interest rates wander, regional rents drop, roofs fail, homes sit empty. What does each design lose?

How it works

Co-ops of 6 to 24 homes are bought only if their income covers their loan payments 1.15 times over. Each picks its own savings rate, pooled share and member credits from a menu, unless you fix them here. A shortfall is met from the co-op's reserve, then a one-off assessment on members, then refinancing, then the pool (separate co-ops can draw up to a quarter of the pool, or four times what they've put in, in a year), or the co-op fails and its lender takes the building. "One balance sheet" pools everything with no limits and one loan, so one default takes all. A mechanism model, not a forecast: the full description and results are in Results, section 4.
